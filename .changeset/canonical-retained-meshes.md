Retained meshes now have a canonical topology. The SMLib kernel listed the vertices and triangles of a Boolean result in an order that changed from run to run, could triangulate a flat face differently each time, and its coordinates themselves can differ by about one unit in the last place between runs. The retained adapter now sorts vertices and triangles, starts each triangle at its smallest vertex, and retriangulates every flat face from its outline. Every ordering and tie-break uses coordinates on a fine grid, so the same model gives the same triangles, vertex order and face keys every run, even when the kernel's last bits differ. The positions themselves are the kernel's and are never rounded, so retained mesh bytes can still differ by those last bits.

The kernel's own triangulation is still kept, so it can still change from run to run, for a curved face other than a cylinder, a cylinder triangle pair whose diagonal the adapter's existing alignment or chord checks leave alone or whose two diagonals are equally long on the adapter's grid (the flip now needs a strictly shorter grid length), and a flat face the retriangulation declines (more than 256 vertices, non-manifold edges, or a result that does not reproduce its triangle count and area; a slightly warped face marked flat therefore keeps its split). The vertex order of a flat face smaller than the grid step (about 61 µm at a 2.6e6 m georeferenced offset) still follows the kernel. Native and WebAssembly builds can order a mesh differently where their coordinates differ by more than the grid step. A zero-thickness part whose coincident back-to-back flat faces would share one split keeps the kernel's flat-face split, as before, so it is published as before rather than made non-manifold.

Index arrays differ from earlier releases: vertex and triangle order changes, and flat faces can be split along different diagonals. Positions, face keys and per-face normal directions are unchanged. Per-face areas are unchanged to within 1e-9 relative, or, for a face so thin that the coordinates' last bits decide it, to within those last bits times its size (about 5e-10 m² per metre of face per triangle at a 2.6e6 m georeferenced offset). Flat normals are recomputed from the longest of each triangle's three corner cross products, so a thin sliver keeps its true orientation. The default exact engine and default wasm are unchanged.
