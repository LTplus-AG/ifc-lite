Reconstruct real model edges in the snap cache, so a snapped edge is the edge a user sees.

`buildGeometryCache` (the geometry behind measure snapping and the add-element tool, reached through `Renderer.raycastSceneMagnetic` -> `SnapDetector.detectMagneticSnap`) pushed one cache entry per triangle-pair edge and merged nothing. A straight model edge crossed by several triangles therefore arrived as several unrelated segments, and `SnapTarget.metadata.vertices` described the segment under the cursor rather than the edge on the drawing. Measured through the real wasm pipeline on `apps/viewer/public/samples/building-architecture.ifc`: IfcSlab #52 carries a continuous 2.000 m opening edge at x = 5.600, y = 0.000, z = -5.000 .. -3.000, and the cache held it as four coincident/adjacent fragments of 0.200 / 1.800 / 1.600 / 0.200. No cursor position anywhere on that edge produced 2.000, and the other samples carried the same class of per-piece fragmentation.

Where two fragments overlapped, the winner was a distance TIE broken only by cache array order: at cursor (5.62, 0, -4.5) the 1.800 and 1.600 fragments both returned the bit-identical distance 0.019999901652336227, and `nearbyEdges.sort((a, b) => a.distance - b.distance)` over a stable sort handed the win to whichever triangle the mesher emitted first. That is the #2388 class — a user-visible number decided by triangle emission order.

Three stages fix it, in order, because each makes the next answerable.

**Weld by position first.** The wasm mesh is unwelded across creases on purpose (`rust/geometry/src/mesh_weld.rs` keys vertex identity on exact f32 position bits plus quantized normal and UV, so flat shading survives), while the cache keyed edge identity on vertex INDEX. One physical edge therefore arrived as two independent map entries with one adjacent triangle each, so every edge classified as a "boundary" edge and the coplanarity test below could never fire on real geometry: 100% of cache edges came from the boundary branch on all three committed samples, every edge was in the cache about twice (2.00 / 1.99 / 1.99), and 911 of the 1755 distinct edges of `building-architecture.ifc` were triangulation diagonals that a user could snap to and measure. `snap-weld.ts` welds positions through a uniform hash grid that probes the (at most two per axis) cells able to hold a point within tolerance, the pattern `rust/geometry/src/router/voids/prism_cut/vertex_dedup.rs` uses in the kernel, so a pair straddling a cell boundary cannot be silently dropped the way a plain `Math.floor` bucket drops it.

**Tolerance.** One value for every stage, a perpendicular DISTANCE and never an angle: `max(1/65536, maxAbsLocalCoord * 2^-22)`, about 15.3 um at building scale. `2^-22` is the f32 relative quantum already used by `F32_ULP_SCALE` in `packages/clash/src/analysis.ts` and by `rust/geometry/src/kernel/retriangulate_cleanup.rs`; the 1/65536 floor is the CSG kernel's own reconcile-grid resolution, measured in `rust/geometry/src/mesh.rs` as flat in triangle count from 10-50 um and only touching real geometry at ~100 um. It is deliberately not raised to a round 1e-4: `vertex_dedup.rs` records that welding at 1e-4 m collapsed real geometry and took corpus defects from 76 to 90. It is derived from LOCAL coordinate magnitudes because `MeshData.origin` is f64 and cancels in every within-mesh difference, so the f32 quantum lives at the local magnitude, not the world one.

**Merge collinear runs.** `snap-edge-runs.ts` groups segments onto a supporting line and unions their 1D intervals, rather than chaining by vertex: interval union is the only formulation that handles abutment, overlap, exact duplication and reversed orientation with one mechanism, and the overlapping-fragment case above is produced by two faces subdividing one model edge at different interior points, which a chain walk mishandles. A tessellated curve is NOT fused: candidates are tested against the GROUP's seed line rather than against their predecessor, so collinearity error cannot accumulate along a chain of chords, and the finest arc the Rust tessellator can emit (`calculate_circle_segments` clamps to 8 segments, so a 5 mm radius gives a 3.827 mm chord with a 381 um sagitta) sits 25x above the tolerance. The gap tolerance equals the collinearity tolerance rather than a round centimetre, because two openings aligned on one line in a slab are distinct model edges separated by a real gap.

Verified end to end on the shipped pipeline: `building-architecture.ifc` goes from 1879 cache edges to 935, `infra-bridge.ifc` from 20608 to 10249, slab #52's opening edge reads one run of 2.0000 m, and all three samples report zero split runs WITHIN ANY SINGLE `MeshData` PIECE (`pnpm test:snap-edges`, a new node-tests gate). That scope qualifier is load-bearing: merging happens per piece, so the zero is a per-piece claim, not a per-model one - at model scope the samples still carry cross-piece splits, quantified in the residual limit below.

**Behaviour a user will notice.**

- Measured lengths and the highlighted edge now describe the whole visible edge instead of a fragment of it.
- Snap no longer lands on triangulation diagonals across the interior of a flat face.
- Sliding along a straight edge no longer freezes. Every interior split of a run used to score valence 2 (two collinear fragments), meet `MIN_CORNER_VALENCE`, and be declared a corner: the snap point stuck there for centimetres of cursor travel and painted corner rings on nothing. Valence now counts distinct edge LINES at a vertex, so a pure split scores 1.
- Real junctions keep their vertex snap. Runs are merged THROUGH a genuine junction (that is what makes the reported length the visible edge), so the junctions are carried on the merged edge and corner detection consults them, gated along the run exactly as an endpoint is. On slab #52 this preserves the corner at (5.600, 0.000, -4.800) where the opening's horizontal edge and its reveal meet the 2 m edge - without it, that point would lose its snap entirely, because the magnetic path never reaches `findVertices` while an edge is nearby. The `edgeLock.isCorner` flag, however, stays restricted to run ENDPOINTS - its pre-change meaning. Its only consumer (`measureHandlers.updateSnapViz`, owned by open PR #2641) encodes the corner ring's position as `edgeT < 0.5`, a start/end boolean that cannot express a mid-run position and would draw the pulsing ring at a run end up to half the run away from the snapped junction. The junction's snap TYPE and POSITION are exact through `SnapTarget`; the ring at mid-run junctions is deliberately deferred until that consumer can carry a corner position instead of a start/end boolean.
- Equidistant candidates now resolve geometrically instead of by array order: closer wins, then the edge that actually spans the cursor over one clamped to its end, then the longer run, then the endpoints lexicographically. Valences and junctions are computed on the same canonically ordered segment set with a symmetric same-line test - computed on the raw array with an asymmetric first-match test, the valence at a junction (which feeds `isCorner` and the corner confidence) read 3 in one triangle emission order and 4 in the other for identical geometry. With both channels canonical, nothing in the chain can be moved by the mesher; the order-independence guard signature covers valences and junctions, not just endpoints and lengths.
- The coplanar cutoff is live for the first time and is NEAR-EXACT: `1 - 1e-6`, a 0.081 degree cutoff. Measured over every two-manifold welded edge of the committed samples, triangulation diagonals of flat faces sit at dot >= 1 - 8.1e-8 (pure f32 noise) while real creases sit at dot <= 1 - 3.66e-5 (0.49 degrees and steeper), so the cutoff sits inside the empty five-decade gap between the populations. A wide cutoff deletes real geometry: at 0.98 (~11.5 degrees), 1093 real creases vanished from `infra-bridge.ifc` alone - among them a 3.500 m deck edge whose faces meet at 3.617 degrees - and that band is where BIM slopes live (a 2% drainage fall is 1.15 degrees, a 6% ramp 3.4 degrees). Erring toward keeping edges on curved and faceted geometry is the conservative direction for snapping: the worst case of too near-exact a cutoff is a benign extra snap edge on a flat face.

`COPLANAR_THRESHOLD` and the `Math.abs` on its dot were unobservable before this change (widening the constant to 0.5, inverting it to -1, or deleting the `Math.abs` each left the whole renderer suite green, because every measured dot was exactly 1.000000). Both are now pinned from both sides: shallow-crease fixtures at 45 / 3.617 / 2.5 / 0.49 degrees must be KEPT, an exactly coplanar seam (dot 1) and its inconsistently wound twin (dot -1) must be DROPPED.

No public API change: `SnapTarget`, `SnapType`, `SnapOptions`, `SnapDetector`, `MagneticSnapResult` and `EdgeLockInput` keep their shapes, and `metadata.vertices` remains the two endpoints of the snapped edge — now of the whole run, so a length readout built on it is correct by construction. `MeshGeometryCache` is internal and gained `length`, per-endpoint valences and junctions on each edge while losing the write-only `vertexEdges` map and the `toFixed(4)`-keyed `vertexValence` map (whose keys split at zero, since `(-0.00001).toFixed(4)` is `'-0.0000'` and `(0.00001).toFixed(4)` is `'0.0000'`).

Known residual limit, unchanged by this work: merging is within one `MeshData`. Mesh fragmentation routinely splits an element into several pieces, and an edge whose segments landed in two pieces still reports the piece's share. Measured at model scope (pooling every piece's cache edges per file): 40 / 51 / 511 cross-piece split runs remain on hello-wall / building-architecture / infra-bridge - e.g. infra-bridge IfcElement #156 is emitted as 14 pieces and one 9.909 m model edge is split across them, so a 0.096 m fragment of it can still be served. The "zero split runs" the gate asserts is strictly per piece and makes no claim at model scope. Merging across pieces would need an expressId-scoped cache, which collides with the per-occurrence keying added for #1405.
