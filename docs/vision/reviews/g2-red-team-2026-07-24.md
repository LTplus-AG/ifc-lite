# G2 Red-Team Review: Phase 2 "Negative Results" Narrative

Date: 2026-07-24. Reviewer: adversarial review agent (read-only, checked against
primary artifacts, re-ran the B2.1 battery live and decomposed its false
conflicts). Commissioned at the G2 gate to attack the program's own
interpretation of its Phase 2 negative results before they harden into the
record. Verdict summary: two of the three headline "negative results" are
better described as instrument failures discovered late (a saturated rubric; a
leaking corpus) than as informative negatives about the moonshot theses; the
third (B2.1) reports a kill-criterion number whose largest components its
ground truth cannot adjudicate. Decisive follow-up experiments are specified
per finding and are being executed as Phase 3 work items.

---

## 1. B2.3 (M5 constrained decoding): the exam cannot measure what it claims to have measured

**The "quality 1.000" tie is a ceiling artifact, and the rubric is provably
coarse.** Every criterion in
`scripts/moonshot/m5-constrained-decoding/tasks.mjs` is either an exact entity
count or a value with a 5% dead zone (`scoreValue`: score 1 for relative error
<= 0.05). More importantly, the criteria are restatements of numbers printed
verbatim in the brief: T01's criteria are "storeys=1, slabs=1, dx=10, dy=8"
against a brief that says "10 m wide (X) by 8 m deep (Y)". The DSL op
parameters map 1:1 onto the measured quantities (slab `width` -> footprint
dx). The task is therefore structured transcription plus occasional easy
arithmetic (T07: pick any w*h with 3wh=7.5). Placement intent is almost
entirely unmeasured: T04's "3 by 2 grid" is scored as `columns=6` plus overall
height - six columns stacked anywhere score 1.0; T05's "door in the south
wall" is scored as `doors=1, windows=3`; T09's "beams spanning between column
heads" is `beams=6`. The `clashFactor` is admitted near-vacuous (DESIGN.md).
So "quality 1.000 in both arms" means "both arms transcribed the briefs'
numbers"; enormous families of geometrically wrong outputs also score 1.0. The
tie is measured on an instrument with no headroom, which is a different (and
worse) finding than "zero margin".

**n=12 licenses almost nothing.** All 12 task pairs are concordant at ceiling.
Clopper-Pearson on 0/12 discordant pairs gives a 95% upper bound of ~26%
per-task discordance; rule-of-three gives 25%. The data are statistically
consistent with the constrained arm being up to ~25 points per-task better or
worse. Single sample per task, no variance estimate (admitted in DESIGN.md).
"Zero margin" should be stated as "unable to detect any margin smaller than
~25 points on a saturated rubric".

**"Task tier too easy" is not the only live hypothesis, and the narrative
buries the biggest confound: the validator was serialized into the prompt.**
`dsl.mjs` `DSL_SPEC` hands both arms the exact fit rules the validator
enforces ("Fit rules: A-W/2 >= 0.05 and A+W/2 <= wallLength-0.05; S >= 0;
S+H <= wallHeight-0.05"). The "informed baseline" is not merely "the same DSL
spec" - it is the constraint machinery distilled into prose. When a model that
follows instructions is given the checker's rules as text, decode-time
enforcement of those same rules is redundant by construction. This is
precisely the setting M5's premise says will not scale (the full kernel's
semantics do not fit in a prompt), and the exam never left it. Competing
hypotheses the data cannot separate: (a) briefs too easy; (b) rubric ceiling;
(c) all promptable constraints were prompted, so the arms were near-identical
protocols. Hypothesis (c) is arguably an argument against M5's premise at this
constraint complexity - and it is the one the original narrative does not name.

**The repair probe proves less than claimed - and something worrying.** The
one live demonstration of the loop (results.json `repairProbe`) is a brief
demanding "sill at exactly 2.0 m" in a 3 m wall with a 1.4 m window -
infeasible under the fit rule. The "converged" program has `sill: 1.55`: the
loop satisfied the kernel by silently rewriting the user's stated requirement,
and the harness recorded this as success. On an infeasible brief the correct
behavior is to surface infeasibility, not launder it. No quality criterion was
applied to the probe. (Named: constraint laundering.)

**Required tier-2 design changes:** (i) demonstrate rubric headroom first - a
deliberately mediocre proposer must land in (0.3, 0.9), otherwise a second
saturation tells you nothing; (ii) hold some validator/kernel rules out of the
prompt - the M5 thesis is specifically that the kernel conveys unpromptable
information; (iii) three arms, not two: constrained-with-repair vs
validator-filtered best-of-k vs unfiltered sampling, all matched on total
model calls - if filtered best-of-k matches the repair loop, the value is
validator-as-filter, not error-message feedback, a materially different
product conclusion; (iv) N >= 30 briefs and >= 3 samples per brief with a
pre-registered paired-difference CI; (v) include deliberately infeasible
briefs where the scored-correct behavior is reporting infeasibility, plus an
intent-fidelity check (emitted programs must not contradict explicitly stated
parameter values).

**Where the original narrative is right:** margin-credit assignment for the
raw-IFC arm (+0.68 belongs to the DSL/compiler) is correctly done; the
raw-arm failure taxonomy (7/12 zero meshable geometry) is verified in
results.json; leniency/timeout confounds are disclosed.

## 2. B2.2 (World Gym benchmark): "add geometric defects" does not fix a benchmark whose answer key is public

**The heuristic's 1.0 is label leakage, full stop.** Every one of the 7
defects has an absolute textual signature that exists only because the
corruption process planted it: `IfcFooting` is a type no clean family emits
and carries literal names `GymClashA-${k}`/`GymClashB-${k}`; degenerate
columns are named `GymDegenerate-${k}` and leave a `,0.);` extrusion suffix;
dangling-ref is the literal constant `#99999999`. The heuristic even encodes
corpus-private knowledge (the "deleted site leaves exactly one dangling id"
budget is a fact about this corruption code, not about IFC). F1=1.0 with 0 FP
over 681 clean and 319 corrupted models is perfect separability of an
injection fingerprint. The labels are a computable function of textual
signatures by construction.

**The deeper structural break: the clean twin is legally reconstructible.**
The corpus is `generateModel(seed, 'auto', {corruptRate: 0.3})`, open source,
with corruption drawn from a separate `{seed}:corrupt` RNG stream, so the
underlying build is unchanged. The rules forbid reading `model.defects` - but
calling `generateModel(seed, family, {corruptRate: 0})` and diffing bytes
reads no plant record and no ground-truth field. That diff recovers every
text-level defect exactly, reveals build-time injections as added lines, and -
because the clean twin embeds the true GymQuantities - also yields
quantity-estimation ~1.0, beating both anchors. Geometric/organic defect
families fall to the same attack: any defect, of any modality, is visible as a
diff against the regenerable clean twin. With a fully open deterministic
generator keyed only by seed, the answer key is public no matter what the
rules say. v1.1 must change the integrity model - secret per-split generation
salt, hosted episode bytes with no local regeneration, or defects planted in
real third-party models that have no twin - not just the defect list.

**Two more design defects:** (a) validity-triage is derivative of
defect-detection (truth severity = count of distinct defect types; given
perfect detection, concordance 1.0 is automatic), so the aggregate
double-counts the leaked task. (b) The claimed quantity-estimation "headroom"
is asserted, never demonstrated: both baselines score an identical 0.977655
because both only read surviving embedded values; no baseline exercises the
geometry-recovery route the spec claims as deliberate headroom.

**Where the original narrative is right:** the dangling-ref finding is a
genuine product gap (oracle F1=0 on 94 planted instances, honestly scored);
catching saturation before public launch is real.

## 3. The "common thread" framing: partially earned, materially survivorship-biased

The harnesses did surface these results. But every one was authored by the
same program, grading distributions the same program generated. Classes of
error they structurally cannot catch:

1. **Common-mode kernel errors.** M5's per-op gate and its quality scorer
   share the identical stack (kernel.mjs re-exports the world-gym check
   module; the selftest validates the harness against itself). A mesher bug
   would move the gate, the measurement, and the selftest coherently, and
   1.000 would stand. (M2 avoids this by scoring against generation-time
   truth; M5 does not.)
2. **Intent-fidelity failure.** Nothing in Phase 2 measures whether an output
   is the building that was asked for beyond enumerated numeric criteria. The
   concrete demonstration already happened: the repair probe rewrote "sill
   exactly 2.0" to 1.55 and was scored a success. A constrained decoder that
   systematically mutates user constraints into whatever the kernel accepts
   would sweep every Phase 2 metric.
3. **External-validity failure.** All three negative results were measured on
   self-authored synthetic distributions (two procedural families, 12
   self-written briefs, op schedules over unit triangles with strictly private
   psets). No harness in the program can detect that real Revit/Tekla files,
   real briefs, or real merge traces violate the generative assumptions.

## 4. B2.1 (merge battery): not literally circular, but the spatial half of the predicate is unfalsifiable under this ground truth

**Circularity check: passes the letter.** `attemptBothOrders` replays through
`applyOps` and compares `canonicalStateBytes` without consulting
`conflictPredicate`; the battery's truth is computed independently of the
predicate under test.

**But the ground truth is defined over an op model that cannot represent the
hazards the spatial rule exists for - verified empirically.** Re-running the
exact battery (seed 20260724) reproduces 10.63% (101/950). Decomposing the 151
flags by which rule fired: of 101 false conflicts, 65 structural-only, 35
spatial-only, 1 both (of the structural ones, 56/66 share no direct node -
they conflict only through the element-ancestor closure). Of 50 true
conflicts: 14 structural-only, 36 both, **0 spatial-only**. In 1,000 schedules
the spatial rule alone never produced a single true conflict, because
node-disjoint ops always commute byte-identically in merge-model.ts
(application is purely per-node; no op can fail or diverge due to spatial
overlap). Every spatial-only flag is scored "false" by definition: ~35% of the
reported false-conflict mass is the entire measurable output of the spatial
predicate, counted against it by a ground truth that cannot see space.
Symmetrically, the headline "zero unsound auto-merges" is near-vacuous under
this op model.

**Would 10.6% survive real traces? Almost certainly not, in the bad
direction.** The synthetic base gives every entity private psets and no shared
nodes; real IFC has shared property sets, type objects, and relationship
fan-out that the ancestor-closure mechanism (already 56/101 of false
conflicts) amplifies. Real sessions cluster edits. The plan's kill criterion
is stated "on real traces"; the battery measures a different quantity on a
distribution engineered (3 m grid spacing) to keep conflicts rare.

## 5. Verdicts

- **B2.3 "zero margin; task tier too easy": plausible but under-determined.**
  Supportable conclusion is stronger and different: this exam design cannot
  measure the M5 midterm quantity. Decisive experiment: three budget-matched
  arms (repair loop / validator-filtered best-of-k / unfiltered) on >= 30
  briefs with constraints partially held out of the prompt, on a rubric first
  shown to have headroom. Threshold: paired CI lower bound > 0.05 vindicates
  decode-time feedback; CI containing 0 while the unfiltered arm scores < 0.9
  means the value is the DSL+validator, and M5's decode-time premise is
  damaged at this constraint complexity.
- **B2.2 "(a) validate gap; (b) fix = geometric defect families": (a)
  supported; (b) diagnosis supported, prescription insufficient.** Root cause
  is the publicly computable answer key (open seed-keyed generator + clean
  twin) and generator fingerprints, not defect modality. Decisive experiment:
  implement the clean-twin-diff submission and score it on dev. Threshold: if
<!-- numeral-ok: 0.9313 :: the oracle-kernel anchor's aggregate through the M2
     benchmark scorer, emitted under benchmark/, outside this artifact set. -->
  it exceeds oracle-kernel's 0.9313 aggregate (prediction ~1.0 including
  quantities), v1.1 must change the integrity model, decouple triage from
  detection, and demonstrate (not assert) quantity headroom.
- **B2.1 caveat: understated.** Decisive experiment: spatially coupled apply
  semantics (hosted openings must remain inside host walls; CSG re-cut on
  geometry moves) and/or replay of real captured multi-user traces; measure
  false-conflict rate restricted to schedules where the spatial rule fired,
  against the plan's < 20% bar.

**Overall:** the program's strongest genuine asset is exactly what it claims -
harnesses that publish their own bad news. But the "verifiable environments"
property is demonstrated for internal consistency only; nothing in Phase 2 yet
verifies anything against the external world.
